### SIM 1 — Achromatic Physics: Grey Has No Wavelength

**Claim:** Grey is achromatic — it has no dominant wavelength, only luminance — making it physically unique in the color canon and physically grounding the Silver Tablet’s doctrine of non-interference.

**Verification:**

Colorimetry and the CIE color model:
- **Chromatic colors** have a dominant wavelength — the single wavelength of monochromatic light that, mixed with white, most closely matches the perceived hue. Every spectral color (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet) has a dominant wavelength.
- **Achromatic colors** (black, white, grey) have no dominant wavelength. They are defined entirely by their luminance factor — the proportion of light reflected relative to a perfect white reference.
- Grey specifically: luminance factor between 0 (black) and 1 (white), no hue component, no chromatic influence on adjacent colors.
- Because grey has no dominant wavelength, it exerts no chromatic pressure on neighboring colors in a palette. It is the only tone that can sit beside any hue without shifting the perceived hue of that neighbor.

This is the physics behind the Silver Tablet’s doctrine: grey witnesses without distorting. It cannot distort chromatically because it has no chroma.

**Result: Achromatic doctrine verified. Grey’s non-interference property is physically grounded in colorimetry.**

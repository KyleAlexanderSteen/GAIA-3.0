### SIM 3 — Calibrated Confidence vs. Plausible Certainty Distinction

**Claim:** Calibrated confidence and plausible certainty are structurally different properties of an intelligence system, and this distinction is canon-level.

**Verification criterion:** The distinction must be traceable to C131 (GAIA Charter) and must map to a testable behavioural difference.

- C131 commits GAIA to truth without dissimulation as its constitutional baseline.
- Plausible certainty is dissimulation in the domain of confidence — it presents a level of surety that the evidence does not support, because the system has prioritised fluency over accuracy.
- Calibrated confidence presents the actual evidence state including its gaps, errors, and uncertainties.
- The behavioural difference is testable: a calibrated system degrades gracefully under new evidence; a system in plausible-certainty mode resists correction and generates credibility damage over time.

**Result: Distinction verified against C131 and against standard epistemological criteria. The commitment to calibrated confidence is a constitutional obligation, not a stylistic preference.**

### SIM 3 — Buddhist Robe Doctrine Verification

**Claim:** The Theravada Buddhist monk’s robe (*kashaya*) is orange-saffron, and this is structurally consonant with the Ember Tablet’s doctrine of disciplined fire.

**Verification:**

Historical record: The *kashaya* robe in Theravada Buddhism is traditionally dyed with natural saffron or turmeric, producing shades of orange and yellow-orange. The color was chosen partly for its availability from natural dyes and partly for its symbolic resonance with fire, warmth, and the burning away of attachment (*upadana*).

Doctrinal mapping: The Theravada tradition specifically frames monastic renunciation not as the extinction of vitality but as the redirection of it — fire that no longer needs fresh fuel because it has become self-sustaining. **Result: Historical and doctrinal verification confirmed. The Buddhist robe doctrine is grounded in the actual practice and symbolism of the Theravada tradition.**
